# Current Status

This page summarizes the present state of the investigation. Unlike the Research Log, which records the research chronologically, this document reflects the current understanding of the project.

---

## Current Phase

**Phase IV — Historical Investigation**

The theoretical framework has been established, and the focus has shifted toward understanding the historical origin of the commonly reported electron gain enthalpy values for argon and krypton.

---

## Current Research Question

> **Where did the commonly quoted value of approximately +96 kJ/mol first originate?**

Although the value appears throughout educational resources, its original source has not yet been identified.

---

## Verified Results

🟢 The major electron-affinity review literature does **not** assign positive electron affinities to argon or krypton.

🟢 Pritchard (1953) does not report positive electron affinities for either atom.

🟢 Berry (1969) does not report positive electron affinities for either atom.

🟢 Hotop & Lineberger (1975) recommend:

> EA(Ar) < 0

> EA(Kr) < 0

🟢 Hotop & Lineberger (1985) maintain the same conclusion while discussing metastable excited states.

---

## Working Hypotheses

🟡 The commonly reported +96 kJ/mol value originated from a later educational interpretation rather than the foundational experimental literature.

🟡 Multiple textbooks may have propagated the same value without citing its original source.

---

## Open Questions

- When did the +96 kJ/mol value first appear?
- Which publication introduced it?
- Is the value experimental, theoretical, or derived through convention?
- How did it become widespread in chemistry education?

---

## Current Direction

The investigation is now focused on tracing the historical propagation of the reported value through textbooks, review articles, and reference compilations until its earliest documented appearance can be identified.
